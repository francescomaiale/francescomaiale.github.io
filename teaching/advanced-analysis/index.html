Before you begin

A little background.

Bring measure theory, integration, normed spaces, and elementary functional analysis. You should be comfortable with integration by parts and convergence of functions; the opening chapters introduce the more flexible topologies needed for test-function spaces.

A first pass

Keep track of the space and its dual.

Use a small dictionary as you read: name the test-function space, its topology, the continuous linear functionals acting on it, and the relevant convergence. This makes the later manipulations of distributions much less mysterious.

This is my suggested reading route, rather than an official course syllabus.

Where to start

A route through the notes.

  1. Chapters 1–3 · pp. 6–35

    Choose the topology first

    Follow seminorms and locally convex spaces into the spaces of compactly supported smooth functions. Compare what it means for derivatives to converge on a fixed compact set with what happens when supports are allowed to vary.

    Keep in mind The topology determines which linear functionals count as continuous.

  2. Chapter 4 · pp. 37–62

    Treat derivatives weakly

    Work through distributions, support, derivatives, convolution, and mollification. Test each definition first on an ordinary locally integrable function, then on the Dirac mass, and compare the two cases.

    Keep in mind A distribution acts on a test function; it need not have a pointwise value.

  3. Chapters 5–6 · pp. 63–85

    Transform the problem

    Move between Schwartz functions and tempered distributions before reading the Fourier–Laplace material. Check the sign and normalization of the Fourier transform once, then use that convention consistently in every derivative identity.

    Keep in mind The ambient space matters when extending a transform by duality.

  4. Chapter 7 · pp. 87–106

    Use the toolkit for PDEs

    Follow fundamental solutions, Sobolev spaces, and elliptic regularity. At each result, record the assumptions on the operator and data, the sense in which the equation holds, and the regularity gained.

    Keep in mind A distributional solution is a starting point; improved regularity requires a separate argument.

Read actively

Three questions to carry with you.

Try answering these in your own words. Open a prompt when you want a starting point.

Why does the derivative definition contain a minus sign?

Start with a smooth function and integrate by parts against a compactly supported test function. The absence of a boundary term reveals the rule that extends differentiation to distributions.

What does smoothing preserve, and what does it change?

Convolve a rough function with a smooth approximate identity. Compare regularity, support, and the topology of convergence instead of assuming that smoothing keeps every property unchanged.

How do you read an elliptic regularity statement?

Write three lines: hypotheses on the operator, hypotheses on the right-hand side, and conclusion for the solution. Identify whether the estimate is local or global and which norm appears on each side.
