Before you begin

A little background.

Start with metric spaces, linear algebra, Lebesgue integration, and basic spaces of functions. It helps to know examples of complete normed spaces, but the first chapters build the Hilbert- and Banach-space framework in detail.

A first pass

Choose a route rather than reading all at once.

For variational problems and PDEs, follow Hilbert and Banach spaces into weak compactness, then turn to Chapter 9 on Sobolev spaces. Return to the operator and functional-calculus chapters when a spectral question motivates them.

This is my suggested reading route, rather than an official course syllabus.

Where to start

A route through the notes.

  1. Chapters 1–2 · pp. 7–39

    Learn the geometric language

    Compare orthogonal projection and Hilbert-space duality with the broader Banach-space setting. Keep a finite-dimensional example and an infinite-dimensional sequence space beside each new definition.

    Keep in mind Completeness is a hypothesis that makes limiting arguments possible, not just a label.

  2. Chapters 3–5 · pp. 40–98

    Separate duality from compactness

    Follow Hahn–Banach, uniform boundedness, open mapping, and weak topologies. Write down which space and which topology appear whenever a theorem gives continuity, boundedness, or compactness.

    Keep in mind Norm convergence, weak convergence, and weak-star convergence are distinct notions.

  3. Chapters 6–8 · pp. 100–158

    Study operators through structure

    Read compact operators and spectral theory before functional calculus and Fredholm operators. Compare the finite-dimensional eigenvalue picture with the assumptions that allow an infinite-dimensional counterpart.

    Keep in mind Not every bounded operator has the spectral behavior of a matrix.

  4. Chapter 9 · pp. 162–189

    Return to functions and derivatives

    Begin with weak derivatives, then follow extension, compactness, and higher-order Sobolev spaces. For each embedding or compactness statement, record the domain and exponents before applying it to a sequence.

    Keep in mind A bound in one function-space norm may yield convergence only in a different, weaker norm.

Read actively

Three questions to carry with you.

Try answering these in your own words. Open a prompt when you want a starting point.

How can a bounded sequence fail to converge strongly?

Use an orthonormal sequence in an infinite-dimensional Hilbert space. Compare the distance between distinct terms with their scalar products against a fixed vector, then connect the example to weak convergence.

What is a duality pairing actually pairing?

Name the space containing the functional and the space containing the vector or function. Distinguish evaluation of a functional from an inner product, even when a representation theorem connects them.

How do weak derivatives connect to classical derivatives?

Start with a smooth function and integrate by parts against a compactly supported test function. Then keep the integral identity as the definition when a classical derivative is unavailable.
