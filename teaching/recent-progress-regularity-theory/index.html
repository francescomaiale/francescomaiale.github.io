Before you begin

A little background.

Bring Sobolev spaces, weak derivatives, Hölder and Sobolev inequalities, and weak elliptic equations. Familiarity with variational integrals and the p-Laplacian helps with the nonlinear examples.

A first pass

Begin with the equation and its assumptions.

Keep the Laplace equation as a reference. List what changes in each generalisation: the coefficients, the gradient dependence, or the right-hand side. This gives the estimates a clear role.

This is my suggested reading route, rather than an official course syllabus.

Where to start

A route through the notes.

  1. Model equations

    Separate existence from regularity

    Read the opening divergence-form equations next to their variational examples. Record the growth, ellipticity, and convexity hypotheses. Ask what the weak formulation tells you about a solution, and what extra information the regularity theory seeks.

    Keep in mind A weak solution need not initially have the pointwise derivatives used in a classical equation.

  2. Local energy estimates

    Understand the cutoff calculation

    Use the Caccioppoli argument as a model proof. Follow the choice of test function, the product rule, and the role of ellipticity. Draw the smaller and larger balls to understand why the estimate needs a margin around the region of interest.

    Keep in mind The cutoff localises the argument, and its derivative introduces a scale.

  3. From estimates to better integrability

    Compare linear and nonlinear arguments

    For singular integrals and gradient estimates, identify the input and output norms before reading the inequalities. Then compare the nonlinear estimates with the linear setting.

    Keep in mind Write down the permitted exponents and the dependence of the constants.

  4. Rough data and comparison

    Follow the quantity being controlled

    When measure data and comparison estimates appear, distinguish the solution, its gradient, and the size of the forcing term. Read the concluding estimates as answers to the opening question: how does the quality of the data influence the solution?

    Keep in mind Changing the data class can change the meaning of solution and the kind of estimate available.

Read actively

Three questions to carry with you.

Try answering these in your own words. Open a prompt when you want a starting point.

Why introduce two concentric balls?

The test function can equal one on the inner ball and vanish outside the larger one. Track where its gradient appears and what happens as the gap between the radii shrinks.

What does ellipticity do in an energy estimate?

Locate the term that must control the gradient from below. Compare this with the upper growth bound used on the other side of the estimate.

What exactly improves in a regularity theorem?

State the starting space for the solution and the stronger conclusion. Distinguish continuity of the solution from integrability or continuity of its gradient.
