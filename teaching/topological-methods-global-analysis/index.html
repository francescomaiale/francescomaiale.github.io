Before you begin

A little background.

Start with multivariable calculus, the inverse and implicit function theorems, topology, and compactness. Functional analysis and Sobolev spaces become useful when the discussion moves to infinite-dimensional functionals and dynamical systems. Draw level sets and sublevel sets to keep the geometric meaning visible.

A first pass

Keep existence and counting separate.

First understand why a degree can guarantee a solution. Then follow how changes in the topology of sublevels force critical points. The later index arguments aim at multiplicity, so record precisely when a result proves one critical point or several.

This is my suggested reading route, rather than an official course syllabus.

Where to start

A route through the notes.

  1. Chapters 2–3 · pp. 8–36

    Stabilise a count under deformation

    Read the degree modulo two before the oriented Brouwer degree. Compare regular preimages and their contributions, then track the assumptions that make degree invariant under a homotopy. Check the boundary at every step.

    Keep in mind A zero can escape through the boundary unless the hypotheses prevent it.

  2. Chapters 4–5 · pp. 37–53

    Use a topological obstruction

    Follow the Borsuk and Jordan arguments as applications of the preceding degree properties. Before each proof, write down the map, its domain, and the contradiction being sought. Return to low-dimensional pictures for intuition.

    Keep in mind A picture suggests the obstruction; the degree argument supplies the precise conditions.

  3. Chapters 6–8 · pp. 55–92

    Connect sublevel topology with critical points

    Begin with deformation lemmas and the Palais–Smale condition, then study saddle-point and mountain-pass constructions. Only afterwards compare genus and Lusternik–Schnirelman category as tools for finding several critical points.

    Keep in mind Minimax geometry and compactness perform different tasks in the existence proof.

  4. Chapter 9 · pp. 94–107

    Translate motion into a functional

    Read Hamilton’s principle alongside the Sobolev-space formulation. Derive the first variation, identify the admissible curves, and ask why a weak critical point gives the desired equation under the stated assumptions.

    Keep in mind A stationary trajectory need not minimise the action.

Read actively

Three questions to carry with you.

Try answering these in your own words. Open a prompt when you want a starting point.

Why do degree arguments impose a boundary condition?

Imagine a family of zeros moving toward the edge of the domain. Identify the condition that prevents the target value from meeting the boundary image during the homotopy.

What does the Palais–Smale condition contribute?

Separate a sequence approaching a critical level from the extraction of a convergent subsequence. Explain why a vanishing derivative alone does not supply the needed compactness.

Why can a mountain-pass point fail to be a minimum?

Sketch two low regions separated by a higher barrier. Compare minimising over all points with minimising the maximum along a family of connecting paths.
