Research explained · Numerical analysis

Finding the slowest growth
in a matrix family.

Choose a matrix, apply it, and repeat. Can switching between several matrices produce slower long-term growth than using any one of them alone?

Francesco Paolo Maiale · Based on joint work with Nicola Guglielmi

Read the paperExplore the MATLAB code ↗Try the example ↓

01 · The question

One matrix is
only the beginning.

A matrix describes a linear update. A sequence of updates becomes a matrix product, and its order matters. The lower spectral radius measures the smallest asymptotic growth per step over all choices of these updates.

A concrete example

Switching can therefore beat repeating either matrix alone. The last value is ρ(P)1/8 for the eight-matrix product P in the table.

The mathematical definition

For a finite family ℱ = {A1, …, Am},

ρˇ(ℱ)=limk→∞minP∈Σk(ℱ)‖P‖1k.

Here Σk(ℱ) contains all products of length k, and ‖·‖ is an induced matrix norm. The limit is independent of the chosen norm.

02 · The idea

Search products.
Tighten the bounds.

Exhaustive search grows exponentially. Our Gripenberg-like approach keeps selected products, using their spectral radii for upper bounds and antinorms—nonnegative, concave, positively homogeneous functions on a common invariant cone—for lower bounds. Adaptive variants refine the antinorm as the search proceeds.

The resulting interval makes the remaining uncertainty visible. A promising product alone gives an upper bound; a matching lower bound is what closes the gap.

Scope and convergence

The paper studies real nonnegative matrix families. Convergence results require its hypotheses; nonnegativity alone is insufficient. See §3 for the assumptions. The eigenvector-enriched variant (E) is experimental and has no convergence guarantee.

Repository & documentation ↗

The companion starter script compares the standard (S), adaptive (A), and experimental eigenvector-enriched (E) methods on the transposed matrix pair from the example above.

Downloads use tested revision 181d6b5, including the adaptive-routine fixes merged on 18 October 2026.

  1. Download the corrected MATLAB code · ZIP and extract it.
  2. Download the starter script into that same folder, alongside the repository’s .m files.
  3. In MATLAB, open that folder as the Current Folder. With Optimization Toolbox available, enter:
lower_spectral_radius_example

The default implementation uses linprog. CVX is an optional alternative described in the repository; it is not needed for these steps.

Read the output on the right scale

The starter divides the matrices by scale and reports estimates on both scales. The known reference value is about 1 after normalization and 6.0093 before it.

Related research · New preprint

From matrix products
to continuous-time switching

My new preprint studies the lower Lyapunov exponent of positive switched systems. Explicit and implicit Euler discretizations connect this continuous-time question to discrete matrix families, producing monotone brackets. Invariant faces determine when the explicit bound converges in reducible systems.

The paper develops convergence results under stated hypotheses and includes numerical brackets verified by exact rational checks. Its complete PDF includes the numerical supplement and the distinctions between verified bounds and other experimental estimates.
