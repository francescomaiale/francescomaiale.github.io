01 · Variational methods

Compactness & weak convergence

Compactness lets us extract a convergent subsequence from a family of candidates. Weak convergence tests a sequence against continuous linear functionals; it often provides a useful topology when strong convergence is unavailable.

A question to carry: Which bounds give compactness, and does the limit remain admissible?

Learn the idea

  • Institutions of Analysis

    Lecturer: Pietro Majer

    Start with weak topologies and Sobolev spaces.

  • Calculus of Variations

    Lecturer: Giovanni Alberti

    Follow the role of compactness in existence proofs.

See it in research

  • Optimal reinforcements

    See why a sequence of increasingly fine designs can lead to a relaxed description.

02 · Variational methods

The direct method

To prove that an energy has a minimizer, start with a minimizing sequence, extract a convergent subsequence, and pass to its limit. Compactness, stability of the admissible class, and lower semicontinuity are the essential steps.

A question to carry: What prevents energy from increasing when we pass to the limit?

Learn the idea

  • Calculus of Variations

    Lecturer: Giovanni Alberti

    Begin with the chapter on the direct method.

  • Institutions of Analysis

    Lecturer: Pietro Majer

    Review the functional-analytic background for weak limits.

See it in research

  • Optimal reinforcements

    Connect the existence question to the choice of admissible reinforcement designs.

03 · Variational methods

Relaxation

A minimizing sequence can develop fine structure whose limit is no longer an admissible classical design. Relaxation enlarges the description and assigns an effective energy that captures the best limiting behaviour.

A question to carry: What information about the fine structure must the relaxed design retain?

Learn the idea

  • Calculus of Variations

    Lecturer: Giovanni Alberti

    Read the relaxation examples after the direct method.

See it in research

  • Optimal reinforcements

    The relaxed connected problem keeps both a support and a material density; it does not simply assert the existence of an optimal unweighted wire.

04 · Variational methods

Γ-convergence

Γ-convergence describes the variational limit of a sequence of energies. A lower-bound inequality controls every convergent sequence, while a recovery sequence shows that the limiting energy can be attained asymptotically.

A question to carry: To obtain convergence of minimizers, what additional compactness is available?

Learn the idea

  • Calculus of Variations

    Lecturer: Giovanni Alberti

    The chapter on Γ-convergence develops the definition and applications.

05 · PDEs & free boundaries

Weak solutions

A weak formulation expresses a differential equation through integrals against test functions, often after integration by parts. This allows solutions with fewer classical derivatives and connects PDEs to functional analysis and energy methods.

A question to carry: Which function space makes every term of the weak formulation meaningful?

06 · PDEs & free boundaries

Free boundaries

In a free-boundary problem, the interface is part of the unknown. One studies the equation in the relevant region together with the shape and regularity of the boundary that the solution determines.

A question to carry: What can the equation tell us about the geometry of its interface?

07 · Geometry & measure

Hausdorff measure

Hausdorff measure describes the dimensional size of a set using covers by small sets. The metric matters: changing the ambient geometry changes the meaning of diameter, dimension, and density.

A question to carry: Which metric and dimensional measure are appropriate for the set?

Learn the idea

    Lecturer: Giovanni Alberti

    Begin with Hausdorff measure, then covering and density arguments.

08 · Geometry & measure

Rectifiability

In Euclidean space, a rectifiable set can be covered, apart from a negligible remainder, by countably many Lipschitz images of the appropriate dimension. Intrinsic versions adapt the geometric pieces to spaces such as Heisenberg groups.

A question to carry: How do local tangent approximations lead to a global geometric description?

Learn the idea

  • Geometric Measure Theory

    Lecturer: Giovanni Alberti

    Read Lipschitz maps, area and coarea, and rectifiable sets before the research explainer.

See it in research

  • Beyond tangent planes

    Explore a sufficient criterion using tangent paraboloids, density, and higher intrinsic regularity.

09 · Matrix families

Spectral radius & matrix products

The spectral radius of one matrix is the largest absolute value of its eigenvalues. For a family of matrices, the order of multiplication matters; the lower spectral radius asks how slowly long products can grow asymptotically.

A question to carry: Why can the behaviour of products differ from that of each individual matrix?

Learn the idea

10 · Matrix families

Antinorms & growth bounds

On an invariant cone, an antinorm is a nonnegative, positively homogeneous, concave function. Under the relevant hypotheses, antinorms provide lower bounds for the growth of matrix products, complementing upper bounds obtained from finite products.

A question to carry: When do the lower and upper bounds justify stopping the search?
