Research explained · Positive switched systems

How slowly can
a switching system grow?

A system can follow several rules, changing between them as time passes. Can we put reliable lower and upper bounds on the slowest long-term growth that switching can achieve?

Francesco Paolo Maiale · Based on my 2026 preprint

Read the complete preprintTry the step-size example ↓Code & verification ↓

01 · The question

Choose a rule.
Let time pass.

Think of several linear laws of motion, each represented by a matrix. At any moment, one law is active. A switching signal chooses which law to use and when to change it.

The paper studies positive switched systems: each mode has nonnegative off-diagonal entries, so a nonnegative state stays nonnegative. Such matrices are called Metzler matrices.

The lower Lyapunov exponent measures the best asymptotic growth rate achievable by choosing the switching. Here “best” means slowest, starting from a fixed strictly positive state. A positive rate still means growth; a negative rate means decay.

The continuous-time model
x˙(t)=Aσ(t)x(t)

The signal σ selects one matrix from a finite family. The exponent concerns long-time behaviour; it is not the growth after just one short step.

02 · The idea

Two discretizations.
One bracket.

Replace a short interval of continuous evolution by a matrix update. Explicit Euler uses Bi = I + hAi; implicit Euler uses Ri = (I − hAi)−1. Sequences of time steps become products of these matrices.

This connects the problem to the lower spectral radius, the slowest asymptotic growth per discrete step. The paper converts the two discrete growth factors into rates per unit time:

Explicit lower rate
g(h) = (ρ̌(ℬh) − 1) / h

Implicit upper rate
G(h) = (1 − ρ̌(ℛh)−1) / h

The bracket: g(h) ≤ λ̌ ≤ G(h).

On their admissible step ranges, reducing h improves both exact bounds monotonically. The implicit upper bound converges for every Metzler family. The explicit lower bound converges under sufficient conditions developed in the paper, including individual irreducibility; convergence for arbitrary reducible families remains open.

Which step sizes are allowed?

Explicit Euler requires 0 < h < 1/cA, where cA is the largest of zero and the negatives of all diagonal entries of the mode matrices. If cA = 0, there is no finite upper restriction of this form. Implicit Euler requires hη(Ai) < 1 for every mode, where η is the spectral abscissa, the largest real part of an eigenvalue.

These restrictions ensure the positivity needed by the bounds. A smaller step alone does not remove the need to estimate the discrete lower spectral radii accurately.

03 · An exact example

See the error
shrink with the step.

The interactive diagram uses the pair from §3.4 of the paper with ε = 1/2:

A1 = −11/21/21A2 = 11/21/2−1

Each mode alone has exponential growth rate √5/2 ≈ 1.118. By switching, the lower exponent is λ̌ = 0.5. This value is known analytically, so we can see the discretization error directly.

Write e(h) = h / (1 + √(1 − h²)). Then

g(h) = 0.5 − e(h),   G(h) = 0.5 + e(h).

For small h, each one-sided error is about h/2. Even with these irreducible modes, first-order error cannot generally be improved.

04 · Why the boundary matters

A coordinate can
stay at zero.

The boundary of the nonnegative orthant contains states with one or more zero coordinates. A face fixes which coordinates are allowed to be nonzero. A mode preserves that face when its flow cannot create the missing coordinates.

Behaviour on a face can differ from behaviour starting strictly inside the orthant. The paper’s face formula describes a uniform lower exponent on an invariant cone through the rates of faces it meets, using only modes that preserve each face. A face can matter even when it is not preserved by every mode.

This helps explain two different questions: whether the exact explicit Euler rate converges, and whether a chosen antinorm or finite polytope can establish a sharp lower bound. The paper gives an example where explicit Euler converges but every finite polytope in the specified class remains capped below the true exponent.

Under individual irreducibility, the paper proves first-order convergence of the Euler rates and a convergent scheme using fixed-antinorm Gripenberg bounds, with step size, search tolerance, and depth controlled together. Those guarantees should not be transferred automatically to adaptive variants.

05 · From estimates to checks

Keep the evidence
with the number.

A numerical search can suggest a good bound. A stored certificate gives an independent verifier something concrete to check. The paper combines suitable lower-bound witnesses with upper bounds from implicit words, mixtures, or switching schedules.

For the supplied certified continuous-time brackets, the verification uses exact rational checks. Some verified lower bounds also transfer to smaller time steps. This is stronger evidence than an unexplained decimal produced by a search.

The manuscript separately identifies estimates that remain floating point, and lower bounds from Algorithm (L) that lack independent replay witnesses. The interactive plot above evaluates an exact formula in floating point; it does not replay these certificates.
